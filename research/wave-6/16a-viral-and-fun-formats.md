# Fun and viral workout formats, and what the evidence supports

A wave-6 note for the formats people pick up from social media, so the app can
offer the ones that hold up and leave out the ones that do not. Every entry added
from this note cites a study below.

## 10-20-30 intervals

Gunnarsson and Bangsbo (2012), "The 10-20-30 training concept improves
performance and health profile in moderately trained runners" (J Appl Physiol,
PMID 22556401). Three to five sets of five rounds, each round 30 seconds easy,
20 seconds moderate, 10 seconds near-maximal, with two minutes easy between
sets. Over seven weeks it raised VO2max about 4 percent and cut 1500 m and 5 km
times (21 s and 48 s), on roughly half the usual training volume. A later review
(Bangsbo 2024, Eur J Sport Sci) reports similar gains in untrained and clinical
groups.

The app already had short-interval and Tabata-style sessions but not this format,
so `ten-twenty-thirty-35` adds it. The 10-second bursts are short enough to stay
sharp, which is the whole point of the format.

## Rucking (walking with a weighted pack)

Knapik, Harman, Steelman and Graham (2012), "A systematic review of the effects
of physical training on load carriage performance" (J Strength Cond Res, PMID
22130400). Combined progressive resistance training, aerobic training and
load-carriage practice produced the largest gains. Load-carriage research
(Patton 1991; Bhambhani 1997, 2000; Boffey 2019) shows carrying 15 to 30 percent
of body mass during walking raises oxygen demand about 10 to 30 percent over
unloaded walking at the same pace, a real step up in aerobic effort without
running.

For recreational use the injury literature (Knapik, Reynolds and Harman 2004)
points to keeping loads under about 20 percent of body mass and progressing over
weeks, not days. `ruck-walk-40` is written to that guidance.

## Low-intensity walking (the cozy cardio idea)

The viral "cozy cardio" and "hot girl walk" formats are low-intensity steady
walking at home. The trend itself is not the evidence; the modality is. Brisk
walking has solid cardiovascular and metabolic support (Hanson and Jones 2015,
walking-groups meta-analysis, Br J Sports Med), and it is the low-impact
counterpart to the running in the plan. `cozy-cardio-30` names the format but
the prescription is plain easy walking.

## Left out

- Exact social-media protocols with no study behind them (for example the precise
  "12-3-30" numbers are one small 2025 study, and the app already carries an
  incline walk, so it is not added as a separate entry).
- Anything whose components the app does not yet render.
